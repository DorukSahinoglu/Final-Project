\section{Results of the NSGA-II and Comparison with Exact Solutions}

The objective of this section is to evaluate the performance of the proposed NSGA-II framework relative to exact results. Exact methods provide a reliable benchmark by guaranteeing global optimality for small instances. However these optimization methods gives a single solution that minimizes cost under constraints and does not give alternative solutions between conflicting performance measures.\par

In this section, exact solutions are used as reference point to calculate capability of the NSGA-II algorithm. Specifically to identify if the proposed metaheuristic can find the optimal cost and generate alternative solutions which improves other performance metrics at the expense of total cost.\par

To test this, NSGA-II is used on the same problem instance used in exact optimization method and the resulting non-dominated solution set is analyzed. Solutions are selected from Pareto frontier to show differences between trade-offs. This genetic framework, allows a comparison between single-objective exact optimization and multi-objective optimization in terms of solution quality.
\subsection{20 x 20 Instance}

This section presents a detailed comparison between exact results and solutions generated by NSGA-II framework for the 20 X 20 employee drop-off instance. The comparison has two purpose. First is to check the capability of metaheuristic algorithm to detect global optimal cost solutions. Second is to investigate the ability of NSGA-II's finding alternative efficient solutions.\par
The exact model minimizes total transportation cost under capacity constraints. While it provides guarantees cost optimality, it does not provide information about potential trade-offs. \par
For this instance NSGA-II is used with the same problem data as the exact model. The algorithm parameters are fixed across all runs. Specifically a crossover rate of 0.9 and a mutation rate of 0.04 are employed. The population size is 40 İs evolved over 10000 generations. Fine tuning of these parameters will be shown at further sections.\par
Comparison focuses on three objective values: Total transportation cost ($f_1$), maximum route duration ($f_2$) and average route duration ($f_3$). From the resulting Pareto-set representative solutions are selected to evaluate trade-offs. These solutions are compared directly with the exact optimal solution.

\subsubsection{Discussion of Results}
The comparison results show that the proposed NSGA-II is capable of identifying global optimal solution in terms of total transportation cost. The cost-optimal solution found by NSGA-II is directly matching with the one from exact optimization. \par

More importantly, NSGA-II generates a set of alternative efficient solutions that significantly improve other performance metrics. Compared to exact cost-optimal solution, balanced and time-focused solutions reduce the maximum route duration from 81 to 65 and lower average travel time by more than 25\%.
These solutions can not be found by single-objective formulations.\par
The results shows the advantages of multi-objective optimization for the employee drop-off problems. Multi-objective methods improves decision-making by offering multiple solutions which balances operational cost and service quality. This becomes more and more important as the problem size grows.

\subsection{25 x 25 Instance}
This section presents a detailed comparison between exact optimization results and solutions generated by NSGA-II framework. Although the difference seem small, this instance is significantly harder than the 20 x 20 instance because of the exponentially increasing solution space. \par
Although exact solver is still able to reach global optimality, it requires substantial amount of time, and a large number of advanced cutting plane strategies. For this problem, the NSGA-II algorithm with a population size of 50 is used. The crossover rate is set to 0.9, allowing effective recombination of promising solutions.\par
Also a relatively high mutation rate of 0.05 is used to enhance diversification and reduce the risk of premature convergence. This high mutation rate is especially important for this instance, because of dense constraint limitations. The algorithm is executed for 10000 generations enabling it to converge to higher quality solutions.

\subsubsection{Discussion of Results}
The results obtained for 25 x 25 instance provide us insight into the behavior of this problem under multi-objective framework. This instance serves as a valuable benchmark for evaluating both quality and diversity.\par
One of the key observations is that identifying near-optimal cost solutions in a multi-objective heuristics is more difficult than identifying in single-objective optimization. Unlike exact cost minimization, this algorithm is not only guided towards a global minimum transportation cost, but it tries to create a set of effective solutions. In this context, achieving a solution with a total transportation cost that is 1.2\% higher than the exact optimum can be considered a strong results, especially in the presence of multiple objectives.\par
Beyond cost optimization, as mentioned earlier, the most significant advantage of NSGA-II is to find alternative solutions. While the exact optimization provides a single cost-optimal solution, NSGA-II generated a diverse Pareto set which includes solutions with different metrics. Several of these alternatives are shown on the table. \par
In the field, such alternative solutions may be preferable even if they have a higher total transportation cost. Reductions in average travel time, directly improve employee comfort and satisfaction, while lower maximum route durations, avoids long trips for individual employees. In real-world planning, these factors can be as important as cost.\par
This instance shows the strength of NSGA-II framework while producing near-optimal solutions, alongside of its capability of producing a rich set of alternative non-dominated solutions.

\subsection{Scalability Analysis using a 66-node VRPLIB Instance}
Up to this point, the analysis has focused directly on the employee drop-off problem, where each customer represents an employee to be transported from a depot. The problem instances reflect the characteristic of the real-world application considered in the earlier sections of this study and solved up to 30 x 30 scale using exact and metaheuristic methods.\par

To further evaluate the robustness and scalability of the genetic metaheuristic framework, a larger benchmark with 66 nodes is used. Unlike other cases, this instance is taken from VRPLIB and corresponds to classical capacitated vehicle routing problem (CVRP). In this problem, each customer has a demand and objective is to minimize total cost. As a result, the structure of the problem differs from the employee drop-off settings and other objectives like average time are no longer modeled.\par

For this instance a modified version of the proposed algorithm is used. While the overall genetic framework, representation of solutions and genetic operators are still te same, tour decoding and feasibility procedures are adapted to match the instance from VRPLIB. Especially capacity constraints ane routing rules became specific to CVRP. \par

The purpose of this experiment is not to outperform other CVRP heuristics specialized for CVRP, but to evaluate the general capability and scalability of the algorithm when applied to a larger and different routing problem.

\subsubsection{Discussion of Results}

The results obtained for the 66-node instance provide valuable insights about the proposed genetic framework. \par

One important observation is that achieving a near-optimal solution on this instance which is particularly challenging for this algorithm. The framework is originally designed as a multi-objective genetic algorithm and its main focus it to balance conflicting criteria. When applied to a single objective CVRP, the algorithm does not have the benefit of specialized cost-focused local search operators like other metaheuristics designed to solve these problems. Despite this problem, the algorithm is able to find a solution with a 2.28\% optimality gap above the optimal solution, indicating that the algorithm has a strong search capability.\par

The results is especially notable considering the complexity of the search space. As the number of customers grow, the feasible route combinations grows rapidly, making it difficult to define routes inside the permutation. While specialized CVRP heuristics are made to exploit problem-specific structures, our algorithm relies on more general genetic mechanisms. The fact that it can still reach near-optimal regions highlights the effectiveness of dynamic-mutation and penalizing system inside the algorithm.\par

It should be noted that the optimality gap can be caused by the absence of deep route-based mechanisms, such as large neighborhood search or local improvement heuristics, however this is a consequence of maintaining the flexible and multi-objective focused structure rather than focusing on a problem-specific solution.\par

The results confirm that the proposed algorithm is not only effective in multi-objective routing problems, but it is capable of generating high-quality solutions on large single-objective instances. 

\subsection{Computational Results of Bloodhound}

This part gives the main numerical results obtained with BloodhoundTest3.
The table is kept more compact than the NSGA-II result section, because here the algorithm has a single main objective value.
For the homogeneous CMT instances, the reference value is the known optimum or benchmark value used in the experiment.
For the heterogeneous instances, the reference value was obtained by solving a simpler decomposition model with Gurobi.
In that model, a cluster-first route-second logic was used.
Several cluster counts and different clustering alternatives were tested, and the best Gurobi result among these trials was selected as the reference value.
So, for heterogeneous cases, the value in the reference column should be read carefully.
It is a useful baseline, but it is not necessarily the true mathematical optimum of the full integrated heterogeneous routing problem.
This is a bit important, because Bloodhound can sometimes pass that baseline, and this does not mean that it beats a proven optimum.
It mostly means that the cluster-first decomposition may have fixed customer groups in a way that excludes the true global best routing structure.

\[
\text{Gap}=\frac{f_{BH}-f_{ref}}{f_{ref}}
\]
where $f_{BH}$ is the BloodhoundTest3 objective value and $f_{ref}$ is the reference value.
For the heterogeneous instances, if BloodhoundTest3 gives a lower value than the cluster-first route-second Gurobi baseline, the gap is written as approximately zero because the baseline itself is not exact optimal.

The homogeneous results show that BloodhoundTest3 is able to reach good values on classical CVRP-like instances.
For CMT01, the algorithm reaches the reference value exactly.
For the larger homogeneous instances, the gap increases slowly from CMT02 to CMT05.
This is not very surprising.
These instances have identical vehicles, and a part of BloodhoundTest3 is actually designed for vehicle heterogeneity and structural constraint handling.
Because of this, some operators do not give their full benefit in these cases.
Still, the results are useful because they show that the algorithm does not fail on homogeneous problems.
It is just not the most specialized method for them.

The heterogeneous results are more related to the real purpose of the algorithm.
These cases were compared with a Gurobi-based cluster-first route-second baseline.
This baseline first groups the customers and then solves the routing part in a simpler way.
To make this baseline less dependent on one clustering decision, more than one cluster number and more than one clustering structure were tested.
The reported Gurobi value is the best one obtained from these alternatives.
It is a practical method, but since clustering is fixed before routing, some route combinations can be missed.
BloodhoundTest3 does not freeze the cluster boundaries in that way.
It can move customers between route communities, repair route borders, and change vehicle assignments during search.
For this reason the comparison is not exactly the same as comparing against a full exact Gurobi optimum.
It is more like comparing against a simple exact-assisted baseline, even if that baseline was improved by trying several clustering alternatives.

In the 50-node heterogeneous instance, BloodhoundTest3 obtains a lower cost than the cluster-first route-second Gurobi baseline.
This result should be interpreted as Bloodhound finding a better integrated routing structure than the decomposed baseline, not as proving global optimality.
The most likely reason is that the Gurobi experiment solved the problem after clustering, and even with several cluster counts and clustering variants, this decomposition can still miss a route structure where customers move across cluster borders.
For the 120-node and 180-node heterogeneous instances, the gaps are 0.0124 and 0.0219.
These values are small, especially considering that the instances are heterogeneous and the algorithm is not using a full exact model for the whole problem.
The computation time also stays in a usable range for these sizes.

Overall, the result pattern matches the expectation of the algorithm.
BloodhoundTest3 can be used on homogeneous instances, but it is more meaningful for heterogeneous and constrained routing cases.
In homogeneous cases, some parts of the algorithm become almost extra machinery.
In heterogeneous cases, those same parts become useful because the algorithm must decide not only the customer order, but also the route partition and the vehicle type assignment.



\subsection{Overall Discussion}

When the results of NSGA-II and BloodhoundTest3 are considered together, it becomes clear that the two algorithms are not strong in exactly the same way. NSGA-II is more useful when the problem is evaluated with multiple objectives and when the decision maker wants to see different trade-off solutions. BloodhoundTest3, on the other hand, is more cost-focused and route-structure focused. It is designed to push one solution toward a better routing cost, while still using population and diversity mechanisms inside the search. So, the comparison is not simply ``which algorithm is better''. It is more about which algorithm is better for which kind of routing question.\par

In the NSGA-II experiments, the main advantage was the ability to generate alternative solutions. The exact solver gives one optimal solution for the selected objective, but NSGA-II can show several different route plans with different cost, maximum route duration and average route duration values. This is useful in employee drop-off type problems, because the cheapest route plan is not always the best operational plan. For example, a slightly more expensive solution can reduce maximum travel time or create a more balanced service level. In that sense NSGA-II is better when flexibility and trade-off visibility is important.\par

However, NSGA-II is not very specialized for single-objective CVRP cost minimization. This can be seen more clearly when the instance size becomes larger or when the problem is mostly about finding the lowest routing cost. Since NSGA-II tries to preserve a Pareto set and population diversity, it does not always collapse directly toward the cheapest route structure. This is good for multi-objective analysis, but it may slow down pure cost optimization. So, for a single-objective routing table, NSGA-II can still find good solutions, but it is not the strongest tool if the only target is cost.\par

BloodhoundTest3 behaves differently. It is not mainly trying to produce a Pareto front. Instead, it tries to improve route cost and route structure with stronger route-level mechanisms. The algorithm uses local search, route-pool recombination, pulse micro-HGS, ejection chains, exact route polish and exact two-route repartition. These mechanisms make BloodhoundTest3 more suitable when the question is: ``Can we get a better single routing solution?'' Because of this, BloodhoundTest3 is more meaningful in single-objective HCVRP or CVRP style comparisons than NSGA-II.\par

For homogeneous CMT instances, BloodhoundTest3 gives acceptable results and reaches the reference value on CMT01. After that, the gap increases with instance size. This does not mean the algorithm is useless for homogeneous problems, but it shows that a clean homogeneous CVRP is not its best environment. In homogeneous cases, vehicle assignment becomes nearly irrelevant, and some of the algorithm's heterogeneous-specific mechanisms become extra load. A simpler CVRP-specialized heuristic can be more direct here. NSGA-II also has a similar issue in pure cost settings, because it is more general and not deeply route-specialized. So for homogeneous and single-objective cost-only problems, both algorithms are usable, but neither should be presented as the most classical specialized CVRP solver.\par

The heterogeneous results are where BloodhoundTest3 looks more natural. In these instances, the reference values were obtained by a Gurobi based cluster-first route-second approach. Several cluster numbers and different clustering alternatives were tried, and the best Gurobi result among them was selected. Still, this method fixes customer groups before routing. Because of this, it may miss solutions where customers need to move across cluster borders. BloodhoundTest3 does not have this same fixed-cluster restriction. It can rebuild route communities during search, change route borders and reassign vehicles. This is why in the 50-node heterogeneous instance BloodhoundTest3 can produce a lower value than the Gurobi decomposition baseline.\par

This should not be interpreted as BloodhoundTest3 beating a full exact Gurobi model. That would be a wrong explanation. The better interpretation is that cluster-first route-second decomposition is not guaranteed to find the global optimum of the integrated heterogeneous problem. Even when multiple cluster numbers and different clustering trials are tested, the decomposition can still remove some possible route structures. BloodhoundTest3 may find one of those missing integrated structures because it does not lock the clusters at the beginning.\par

The difference between NSGA-II and BloodhoundTest3 also appears in how they use diversity. NSGA-II uses diversity to keep different trade-off solutions on the Pareto front. This diversity is mostly decision-support oriented. BloodhoundTest3 uses diversity more as a search tool. Its elite pool, artificial archive, pulse genetic search and novelty mechanisms are not mainly for presenting many final alternatives. They are used to avoid premature convergence and to find a better final solution. So both algorithms use population diversity, but the purpose is different.\par

Computation time is another point. NSGA-II can require many generations, especially when the population has to approximate a Pareto front. BloodhoundTest3 also has expensive mechanisms, but they are more targeted. Exact route polish is used only on small routes, and exact two-route repartition is used only on limited customer pools. Therefore, BloodhoundTest3 is heavier than a simple local search but still controlled. In the reported heterogeneous instances, the computation time stays in a reasonable range, especially considering that route and vehicle assignment decisions are handled together.\par

Overall, the experiments suggest a division of roles. NSGA-II is better when the problem is multi-objective and when alternative route plans are valuable. It is useful for showing how cost, maximum route duration and average route duration trade with each other. BloodhoundTest3 is better when the goal is to obtain one strong route solution, especially in heterogeneous or constrained cases. It is more aggressive in route repair and more focused on structural cost improvement.\par

As a final comment, these results should be interpreted with some care. The exact results are reliable for the small or single-objective cases where the full model is solved. But the heterogeneous Gurobi values are decomposition-based baselines, not full integrated optima. Because of this, the heterogeneous comparison mainly shows that BloodhoundTest3 can compete with and sometimes improve over a strong cluster-first route-second baseline. It does not prove global optimality. Still, for practical routing, this is a useful result because real routing systems often use decomposed methods due to time limits and complexity.\par

In short, NSGA-II and BloodhoundTest3 serve different purposes in the project. NSGA-II gives more explanation power for multi-objective decision making. BloodhoundTest3 gives stronger single-objective search behavior for heterogeneous and route-structure-heavy instances. This is why both algorithms are kept in the study, because they answer different optimization needs rather than replacing each other completely.
